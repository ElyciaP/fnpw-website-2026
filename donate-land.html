Donate your land and protect nature for generations

Most gifts to nature are counted in dollars, and a few are counted in hectares. If you own land with conservation value, we can help protect and restore it, and we’ll guide you through every step.

Is my land suitable?

What your land could protect

Land does not need to be spectacular to matter. Some of the most valuable country in Australia is quiet to look at, like a paddock holding the last remnant scrub in the district, or forty hectares of regrowth sitting between two reserves that have waited decades to be joined.

Whether you own bushland, wetlands, farmland or another property with conservation potential, your land could help to:

  • support conservation shaped in partnership with Traditional Custodians
  • extend a national park or protected area
  • reconnect habitat and wildlife corridors that have been broken apart
  • protect the places threatened plants and animals depend on
  • restore country that has been degraded

If you are unsure whether your land counts, that is worth a conversation. We would far rather talk it through and tell you honestly what we think than have you decide on our behalf that it wasn’t worth raising.

Our track record

Land we have helped protect since 1970

For more than five decades we have helped add land to national parks and protected areas across Australia, working alongside Traditional Custodians, landholders and government to see each one through. Much of this work happens in South Australia with the Department for Environment and Water, and we have supported land conservation in Queensland and other parts of the country as well.

  • 600K+ hectares protected for national parks and reserves
  • 50 national parks and reserves created or expanded

What happens

How donating land works

A gift of land is a gift to the nation, which is why we don’t keep it ourselves. The government adds it to the national park estate, where it is protected by law and can’t be sold, and our role is to make sure it gets there. Every gift of land is different and is subject to assessment and to the government’s acceptance, so we will give you a realistic picture of timing once we know your land.

Step 01

Start with a confidential conversation

Tell us where the land is and what you would like for it, with nothing committing you until documents are signed.

Step 02

We assess the land and seek agreement

Our projects team looks at its conservation value, then the national parks agency in your state confirms it wants to receive the land.

Step 03

We coordinate valuation and transfer

We arrange the independent valuation, legal work and transfer with your advisers and the government’s.

Step 04

Your land becomes national park

The government gazettes it as part of the park estate, and we record the hectares your gift has protected.

FAQs

Common questions about donating land

Who will own the land?
The government. Once the transfer is complete, the land becomes part of the national park estate and is managed by the national parks agency in your state. We don’t keep it: where it’s needed, it passes through our Gift Fund Trust only for the moment of transfer.
How much land do I need to have?
There is no minimum. What matters is what is on the land and what is around it. A small block adjoining an existing reserve can be worth more to conservation than a large one in the middle of cleared country. If you are close to a national park, a reserve or a wildlife corridor, please do get in touch, whatever the size.
Will my land definitely be accepted?
Not always, and we would rather say so here than let you find out late. The land needs real conservation value, and the government has to agree to receive it before we go any further. If it isn’t suitable, we will tell you why as early as we can.
Are there tax benefits to donating land?

A gift of land may be tax deductible. Tax law allows an income tax deduction for gifts of property, including land, valued at more than $5,000 by the Commissioner of Taxation, and that deduction can be spread over up to five years.

An independent valuation is required for tax purposes and we coordinate it as part of the process, and we will talk you through the costs involved, including valuation and legal fees, before you commit to anything.

Tax rules for gifts of land depend on your circumstances, so please speak with your own accountant or legal adviser before you commit.

Can I protect my land and keep living on it?
Not through a gift of land with us. Our process transfers ownership, because the land has to pass to the government to become national park, and that is what lets it be gazetted and protected by law. If you are not ready to part with your land yet, we are still glad to talk it through with you, with no obligation, whenever the time feels right.
What happens to my land once it becomes national park?
Once it is gazetted, the land is managed as national park under the same rules as every other park in your state. Tell us early what matters to you about the land and we will share it with the government as part of the conversation. Conditions attached to a gift can also affect its tax treatment, so talk them through with your adviser too.
What if my family have views about this?
They usually do, and that is right and proper. Land carries family history in a way money does not. We would always encourage you to have that conversation before you have it with us, and we are very glad to talk to your family alongside you if that would help. A gift that causes hurt at home is not one we want.
