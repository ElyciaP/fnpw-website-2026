Gifts of land

A gift you can walk through.

Most gifts to nature are counted in dollars. A few are counted in hectares. If you hold land with wild value in it, there is a way to protect it for good.

Placeholder image
Where to begin

Not sure your land counts? That is worth a conversation.

People often assume we are only interested in the spectacular. In truth, some of the most valuable country in Australia is quiet and unremarkable to look at: a paddock with the last remnant scrub in the district, a scrappy gully that happens to hold a creek, forty hectares of regrowth sitting between two reserves that have been waiting decades to be joined up.

There is no cost and no obligation in asking, and no question is too small to put to us. We would far rather have the conversation and tell you honestly what we think than have you decide on our behalf that it was not worth raising.

Some of what we are offered becomes part of a national park. Some is protected while staying in private hands. Some is not right for conservation at all, and there is still a good path for that. We will explain all of it before you commit to anything.

Three ways

It does not have to mean letting go.

Giving land is not one decision. It is three different ones, and people arrive at us wanting quite different things. Start with whichever of these sounds most like you.

“I am ready to hand it on now.”

Give the land in your lifetime

You transfer the property to us, and we take on the cost, the care and the responsibility from that point forward. It is the most direct route, and the only one where you get to see what happens next. It also lifts a genuine weight, which matters to people managing land they love and can no longer look after the way they would like.

Best if you no longer use the land, or you would like the certainty settled while you are here to see it done.
“I want to stay, but I want it safe.”

Protect it, and keep living on it

You do not have to give up your land to protect it. A conservation covenant is registered on the title, so the protection travels with the land to every owner who comes after you. You keep the property, you keep living there, and you can usually keep farming the parts of it that are farmed. Covenants are granted by state bodies rather than by us, and we will point you to the right one.

Best if the land is your home, or you want it to stay in the family with its future settled.
“I want to keep it for now.”

Leave the land in your Will

Nothing changes today. You own and manage the property exactly as you do now, and the transfer happens only when you no longer need it. You can change your mind at any point, for any reason, and you never have to tell us. It is still worth a conversation first, so your executors are not left guessing at the hardest possible moment.

Best if the land is still in use, or the decision feels like one for later. More on gifts in your Will
What happens

Nothing moves faster than you want it to.

This is a considered decision, often the largest single gift a person makes in their life, and it should feel considered the whole way through. Here is the shape of it, so there are no surprises.

How long it takes

A first conversation can happen within days. A visit to the property usually follows within a few weeks. From there, gifts of land generally take some months to complete, because titles, boundaries, access and any environmental checks all take the time they take. Complicated titles take longer. We will give you a realistic estimate early rather than an optimistic one, and we will keep you posted the whole way.

01. A conversation, in confidence

You tell us where the land is, roughly how big it is and what you know about it. Nothing you tell us goes anywhere else, and nothing at this stage commits you to a thing.

02. We come and look

Where we can, we walk the property with you. You know it better than any assessment will, and the things you tell us on that walk, where the water sits, what has come back since the fires, which corner the gliders use, usually matter more than anything on a map.

03. An honest assessment

We look at habitat value, at what sits next door, at condition and access, and at whether we can genuinely fund the care this land will need forever. Then we come back to you and tell you plainly what we think, including if the answer is that another organisation or another pathway would serve the land better.

04. Agreeing what happens to it

Before anything is signed, we agree together what the land is for and where it is headed, whether that is addition to a national park, protection under a covenant, or another route. Your wishes are written down, not assumed, and nothing is ever sold without your agreement.

05. Transfer, and then the long work

Your solicitor and ours handle the conveyancing. After that the slower part begins, and it does not end: weed control, revegetation, monitoring, fire planning, and the partnerships with Traditional Custodians and parks services that keep the place in good hands for good.

The practical part

What it costs, and who pays for what.

Every gift of land is a little different, so treat this as a starting point rather than a fixed rule. If something here does not fit your situation, tell us and we will work it out together.

What we cover

  • The conservation assessment of the property
  • Our own legal and conveyancing costs
  • Any survey work we need to make our decision
  • Every cost of caring for the land from the day it changes hands, for good

What you cover

  • Your own solicitor
  • Your own accountant or financial adviser
  • Any valuation you need for tax purposes
  • Paying out any mortgage or other claim on the title first

If cost is the thing standing in the way, please tell us. Plenty of people who own land worth protecting do not have much spare cash, and we would far rather work out how to share the load than lose the land over legal fees. Where a property has real conservation value, we will look at what we can carry.

What this can look like

One decision, an entire island.

Mundoo Island, South Australia

The gift that closed the gap.

At the Murray Mouth, where the Coorong’s freshwater meets the sea, Mundoo Island had been farmed for more than a century. In 2025 the whole of it, 1,900 hectares of wetland, saltmarsh and coastal habitat, came onto the market in a narrow and time-critical window.

A large part of what secured it came from one person who had decided, years earlier, that what she left behind should go to the natural world. Building on that gift we mobilised the rest, and working alongside the Australian and South Australian Governments, the Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal Corporation and our conservation partners, we secured the whole island and transferred it into Coorong National Park.

Sixty-five nationally threatened species. A refuge on the East Asian-Australasian Flyway. Protected now, and for good.

For thousands of years Mundoo has held deep cultural and spiritual meaning for the Ngarrindjeri people, whose connection to this Country continues to guide its care.

Questions people ask

The things worth asking out loud.

How much land do I need to have?
There is no minimum. What matters is what is on the land and what is around it. A small block adjoining an existing reserve can be worth more to conservation than a large one in the middle of cleared country. If you are close to a national park, a reserve or a wildlife corridor, please do get in touch, whatever the size.
Will you definitely accept it?
Not always, and we would rather say so here than let you find out late. We have to be confident we can protect and care for a property permanently, and that means looking at habitat value, condition, access, what adjoins it and whether the ongoing costs can be met. If we cannot take it on ourselves, we will tell you why, and we will point you towards the organisation or the pathway that suits the land better. Nobody who offers us land is sent away empty-handed.
What if my family have views about this?
They usually do, and that is right and proper. Land carries family history in a way money does not. We would always encourage you to have that conversation before you have it with us, and we are very glad to talk to your family alongside you if that would help. A gift that causes hurt at home is not one we want.
Can I say what the land should be used for?
You can tell us what matters to you and we will take it seriously, and where we can we will write it into what we agree. What we will not do is promise something we cannot guarantee decades from now. Conditions attached to a gift can also affect its tax treatment, which is another reason to talk it through early with us and with your adviser.
What if my land is not right for conservation?
It can still do a great deal of good. Property without conservation value can be gifted and sold, with the full proceeds going into protecting and restoring country elsewhere. Houses, units and blocks of no ecological interest at all have funded serious conservation work. We never sell without agreeing it with you first, and if we think another organisation would serve the land better, we will say so and point you to them.
