Donate land

A gift you can walk through.

Most gifts to nature are counted in dollars. A few are counted in hectares. If you own land with real conservation value, you can give it to become national park, protected for good, and we will guide you through every step.

How a gift of land works

Your gift becomes national park. We make sure it gets there.

A gift of land is a gift to the nation. We work alongside Traditional Owners, landholders and government to see it through, and we don’t keep the land: the government adds it to the national park estate so it is protected for good.

You

Offer the land

You tell us about land you would like to protect, and you decide with your own advisers whether to go ahead. Nothing commits you until the documents are signed.

Us

Make it happen

We assess the land, secure the government’s agreement to take it, and coordinate the valuation, legal work and transfer. Where it’s needed, the land passes through our Gift Fund Trust for the moment of transfer, but we never hold it long term.

Government

Protect it by law

The national parks agency in your state receives the land and formally gazettes it as part of the national park estate. From then on it is protected by law and can’t be sold.

Is my land suitable?

Not sure your land counts? That is worth a conversation.

People often assume we are only interested in the spectacular. In truth, some of the most valuable country in Australia is quiet and unremarkable to look at: a paddock with the last remnant scrub in the district, a scrappy gully that happens to hold a creek, forty hectares of regrowth sitting between two reserves that have been waiting decades to be joined up.

There is no cost and no obligation in asking, and no question is too small to put to us. We would far rather have the conversation and tell you honestly what we think than have you decide on our behalf that it was not worth raising.

Every property is assessed on its conservation value and on whether the government wants it for the national park estate. Not every property will be right, and if yours isn’t, we will tell you honestly and as early as we can.

What happens

Five steps from your land to a national park.

A gift of land is a significant decision, so every step is careful and nothing is rushed. The government agrees to take the land before any valuation or legal work begins.

How long it takes

Every gift of land is different. Government agreement, valuation and the legal work all take time, and we will give you a realistic picture of the timeline once we know your land.

01. We assess your land

It starts with a confidential conversation about where the land is and what you know about it. Our projects team then looks at its biodiversity value, what it connects to and the species it supports, and commissions a biodiversity report if one is needed.

02. We secure the government’s agreement

We approach the government agency that manages national parks where your land is, such as the Department for Environment and Water in South Australia, where we do much of this work, to confirm it wants to receive the land and that it fits its priorities for the park estate. Nothing moves to valuation until the government has agreed in principle.

03. We coordinate the valuation and legal work

We agree the right structure for the gift with you and your advisers, arrange an independent valuation that meets ATO requirements, and prepare the donation agreement and transfer documents with your legal team and the government’s.

04. The land is transferred

The transfer is registered through the Titles Office. Where it’s needed, the land passes through our Gift Fund Trust for the moment of transfer on its way to the government.

05. It becomes national park

The government adds the land to the national park estate and formally gazettes it, so it is protected by law and can’t be sold. We give you the documentation you need and record the hectares your gift has protected.

Tax and costs

A gift that can carry a tax benefit.

Our Gift Fund Trust is a Deductible Gift Recipient on the Register of Environmental Organisations, so a gift of land may be tax deductible. An independent valuation is required for tax purposes, and we coordinate it as part of the process. We will also talk you through the costs involved, including valuation and legal fees, before you commit to anything.

Tax benefits for property donations

Since changes in 1999, the Income Tax Assessment Act:

  • Allows an income tax deduction in relation to gifts of property including land valued at more than $5000 by the Taxation Commissioner to eligible charities, regardless of when and how the property was acquired.
  • Allows apportionment (spread) of deductions over five years in relation to these property donations.

Tax rules for gifts of land are specific, so please get advice from your own accountant or financial adviser before you commit. For more information on tax incentives for property donations for conservation, please visit:

  • The Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water
  • The Australian Taxation Office
Thinking about your will?

Land can’t be left in a will. A gift of money can still protect it.

A gift of land has to be made in your lifetime, through the steps on this page. If you would rather protect land through your will, a gift of money can do that.

It has happened before. A substantial part of our contribution to securing Mundoo Island, at the mouth of the Murray, came from a gift in a will. In 2026 its 1,900 hectares became part of Coorong National Park, on Ngarrindjeri Country.

FAQs

Questions people ask.

Who will own the land?
The government. Once the transfer is complete, the land becomes part of the national park estate and is managed by the national parks agency in your state. We don’t keep it: where it’s needed, it passes through our Gift Fund Trust only for the moment of transfer.
How much land do I need to have?
There is no minimum. What matters is what is on the land and what is around it. A small block adjoining an existing reserve can be worth more to conservation than a large one in the middle of cleared country. If you are close to a national park, a reserve or a wildlife corridor, please do get in touch, whatever the size.
Will my land definitely be accepted?
Not always, and we would rather say so here than let you find out late. The land needs real conservation value, and the government has to agree to receive it before we go any further. If it isn’t suitable, we will tell you why as early as we can.
Can I leave land to you in my will?
No. A gift of land has to be made in your lifetime, through the process on this page. If you would like your will to protect land, a gift of money can do that, and it is how a gift in a will helped secure Mundoo Island. Find out about gifts in wills.
Can I protect my land and keep living on it?
Not through a gift of land with us. Our process transfers ownership, because the land has to pass to the government to become national park, and that is what lets it be gazetted and protected by law. If you are not ready to part with your land yet, we are still glad to talk it through with you, with no obligation, whenever the time feels right.
Can I say what the land should be used for?
Once it is gazetted, the land is managed as national park under the same rules as every other park in your state. Tell us early what matters to you about the land and we will share it with the government as part of the conversation. Conditions attached to a gift can also affect its tax treatment, so talk them through with your adviser too.
What if my family have views about this?
They usually do, and that is right and proper. Land carries family history in a way money does not. We would always encourage you to have that conversation before you have it with us, and we are very glad to talk to your family alongside you if that would help. A gift that causes hurt at home is not one we want.
