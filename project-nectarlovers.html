Nectarlovers

Holbrook region, New South Wales   ·   Since 2019

Five nectar-feeding birds, a landscape that depends on them, and almost no record of where they go.

Figures from our Impact Report 2024.

Nectar-feeding birds hold the inland Grassy Woodlands together, and we know remarkably little about where they actually go. The Painted Honeyeater, Black-chinned Honeyeater, Little Lorikeet, Swift Parrot and Dusky Woodswallow all move across these landscapes following flowering events, and formal records of their presence are thin in exactly the districts that matter most.

Finding out before deciding

We fund this with BirdLife Australia, alongside Holbrook Landcare and the New South Wales Government’s Saving our Species program. The first job is knowledge: landholders and volunteers survey and map the birds on their own properties, which builds the presence records that were missing and builds interest at the same time.

What follows from that is targeted: managing the food resources and the habitat features these birds depend on, particularly hollow-bearing trees and understorey structure, then infill planting at the sites the data says are worth it.

Who else benefits

The same habitat carries the critically endangered Regent Honeyeater, of which somewhere between 250 and 350 birds remain in the wild, along with Grey-headed Flying Foxes, Squirrel Gliders and Brown Treecreepers.

For the landholders, the return is practical. They learn which birds are on their land, they get management guidance that also serves farm sustainability, and they are part of something larger than their own boundary fence.

Where it stands

Fifty-four hectares are now secured through stewardship agreements against a target of a hundred, with $70,000 invested in habitat actions across six projects. More than two hundred people have come through field days and workshops, and the work has reached over 2,500 people through regional events and media. Landcare partners now include West Hume and Corowa District.
