Yarrahapinni Wetlands Restoration

NSW   ·   Since 2019

The Yarrahapinni Wetlands Restoration project revitalizes degraded wetlands, restores biodiversity, and improves water quality in northern New South Wales.

The project

A story of balance restoration

In New South Wales’ Mid North Coast, the Yarrahapinni Wetlands are crucial to the Macleay River catchment. Flood mitigation had caused ecological damage, but the Foundation for National Parks & Wildlife, with stakeholders, restored the ecosystem by removing floodgates and the levee wall, reintroducing tidal flows, and conducting a Cultural Heritage Assessment.

Yarrahapinni Wetlands, a Vital Ecosystem

In the heart of New South Wales’ Mid North Coast lies the Yarrahapinni Wetlands, a gem between Stuarts Point and South West Rocks, nestled within the Yarrahapinni Wetlands National Park. This expansive estuarine wonderland is more than just a picturesque landscape; it’s a crucial piece of the Macleay River catchment, brimming with life and cultural heritage.

The wetlands play a vital role in enhancing the estuarine habitat, boosting water quality, and supporting a diverse array of species, from fish to migratory waders. However, decades ago, the introduction of flood mitigation projects in the 1960s and 70s led to the installation of floodgates in 1972, disrupting the natural cycles and causing environmental damage. This intervention lowered water tables, exposed acid sulphate soils, and increased blackwater events, leading to significant ecological harm.

Reviving Yarrahapinni

Driven by a vision to restore the natural balance of this unique ecosystem, a dedicated restoration project was set into motion. The Foundation for National Parks & Wildlife with a broad coalition of stakeholders, including government agencies such as NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service, local communities, environmental groups and Aboriginal custodians, began the ambitious task of rejuvenating the Yarrahapinni Wetlands.

The completion of the levee wall removal in November 2023 stands as a testament to what can be achieved when science, community, and cultural values align. This project is a shining example of how dedicated efforts can lead to profound and inspiring change, offering hope and renewed vitality to one of Australia’s most treasured landscapes.
