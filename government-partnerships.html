Government partnerships

Trusted by government since 1970 to deliver conservation at landscape scale.

For more than 55 years, Australian, state and territory governments have worked with us to expand protected areas and recover threatened species. We bring Traditional Owners, funding and delivery partners to one table, and we stay with every project until we can show what changed.

Ways to partner

Find the partnership that fits your commitment.

Every partnership is shaped around the outcomes you need to deliver.

01

Fund Conservation at Scale

Fund land for the national reserve system, threatened species recovery or landscape restoration, with the scope and the outcomes it will deliver agreed from the start.

02

Build Conservation Partnerships

Work with us over several years on a priority landscape or species, across state borders where the work needs it, and grow the program as the results come in.

03

Invest in Nature’s Future

We’re exploring how public, philanthropic and private capital could work together on long-term landscape outcomes. This work is still being developed, and we’d welcome governments who want to help shape it.

04

Deliver Grant Programs

We design and run grant programs on your behalf, getting funding to the community organisations, rangers and specialists already doing the work.

How we work

From first idea to final report.

  1. 01
    Identify

    With Traditional Owners, agencies and scientists, we find the priority and shape it into a project that meets your program objectives.

  2. 02
    Deliver

    We bring the partners and co-funding together and engage specialist delivery partners against clear milestones, managing the grants and contracts along the way.

  3. 03
    Demonstrate

    We track ecological, financial and partnership outcomes and report against them, so you can show what the investment achieved.

Conservation and the visitor economy

Tourism budgets can fund conservation too.

Governments are bringing parks and tourism closer together. That opens a second route to fund environmental outcomes, through visitor-economy objectives, and we help design projects that meet both briefs at once.

Epic trails · South Australia · Since 2021

Remarkable Southern Flinders

Tourism and conservation in one project. Alongside the new park, the project is building a nature-based and Aboriginal cultural tourism destination less than three hours from Adelaide.

$8m
project, a collaboration with local and state governments
30,000 ha
joined into one park
150+ km
of singletrack in the Melrose network
Read about the project →
  1. 01An Epic mountain bike trail through Mount Remarkable National Park, designed for international Epic trail accreditation
  2. 02The Gorges Walk, a multi-day hike through Telowie and Nelshaby gorges in Wapma Thura
  3. 0320 km of new mountain bike trail open at Mount Remarkable, and a Melrose network of more than 150 km of singletrack
  4. 04Day visitor facilities at Wirrabara and an upgraded Mambray Creek campground
  5. 05Designed with input from the Nukunu Wapma Thura Aboriginal Corporation
First Nations partnerships

Conservation led by Traditional Owners, backed at scale.

Many of the largest outcomes we have helped deliver sit on Country, with Traditional Owners setting direction and co-managing what is protected. For government, that means projects that deliver cultural, social and biodiversity commitments together.

  • Nukunu Traditional OwnersWapma Thura, Southern Flinders Ranges, SA

    Co-managers of the national park at the heart of Remarkable Southern Flinders.

  • Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal CorporationMundoo Island, Murray Mouth, SA

    Shaping the management plan for the 1,900 ha now being added to Coorong National Park.

  • Warddeken rangersWarddeken Indigenous Protected Area, NT

    Indigenous-led recovery of threatened mammals across 1.4 million hectares.

  • Barengi Gadjin Land CouncilLittle Desert, VIC

    Redeveloping the native nursery and arboretum beside Little Desert National Park, with a focus on cultural heritage.

  • Ngurrawaana rangersMillstream Chichester National Park, WA

    Traditional Owners working with parks staff to protect Pilbara olive python and northern quoll habitat.
