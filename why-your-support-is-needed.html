Why your support matters.

Australia is home to species found nowhere else, and one of the worst extinction records on Earth. Support is not optional; it is the whole mechanism.

Why it matters

Together we are making conservation happen.

We are the charity partner of national parks and wildlife services across Australia. We are a non-government organisation with a mission to safeguard our ecosystems, wilderness and wildlife for future generations.

We believe in conservation, not only because it protects native plants, birds and animals, but also because it protects us. After all, you simply cannot be healthy in an unhealthy environment.

The scale of it

Species in trouble, and lost forever.

In Australia today, millions of hectares of habitat have no permanent protection. Without a lasting form of protection, habitats can be degraded or destroyed, and a high rate of species extinctions is the result.

To date almost 2,000 plant and animal species are in trouble or extinct. Australia has the highest rate of vertebrate mammal extinction in the world, and nearly one in three of our unique mammals is at risk of extinction.

What is driving it

Three pressures, all of them reversible.

Invasive species

Invasive animals are the number one threat, affecting 82% of all threatened species, with cats and foxes the biggest threat to our mammals. Invasive plants are a problem too: escaped garden plants account for over 70% of all environmental weeds.

Habitat clearing

Australia’s native forests now cover only 19% of the total land area, and over 40% of forests and woodlands have been cleared, mostly for agricultural expansion. About 8 million hectares of threatened species’ habitats were cleared between 2000 and 2017.

Fire and climate

Climate change threatens to increase the rate of losses. In New South Wales alone, over 37% of the state’s national park estate was impacted by the Black Summer fires.

Sources: Australian Government threatened species lists for fauna and flora; CSIRO on invasive species; State of the Environment.

Impact you can see

Acting now, as we have since 1970.

We work with local, state and federal governments to gain permanent protection for habitats and species, and to grow Australia’s national parks system. We do that work in partnership with scientists, First Nations and community groups, and with individuals and companies who want the same thing.

55 yearsand counting

Since 1970 we have been protecting land and species for future generations, so that all Australians can enjoy these natural gifts.
