Growing Koala Trees

2024-08-26  ·  2 min read

Australia’s iconic Koala is facing a dire situation due to habitat loss, fragmentation, and degradation. The Foundation for National Parks & Wildlife (FNPW) is stepping in to help reverse this trend through innovative habitat restoration efforts that focus on growing native koala food trees.  

 

 

DOWNLOAD THE infographic

 

 

What’s the Importance of Native Koala Food Trees?  Koalas, listed as endangered under the national Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation (EPBC) Act, rely heavily on specific eucalyptus species for food. However, habitat loss and changes in land use have led to a shortage of these vital koala food trees, particularly those that are of local provenance and best suited for regional Koala populations. The decline in Koala populations can be directly attributed to the destruction of their habitat, which not only reduces food availability but also fragments their living space, making it harder for them to survive and reproduce. 

 

The Community-Based Nursery Growing Koala Native Trees

To tackle the shortage of Koala trees, the Foundation for National Parks & Wildlife runs the Landscape Resilience Program, which includes 30 Community-Based Nurseries. This initiative engages local plant nurseries, such as the Friends of the Koala Nursery in Lismore, NSW, to grow native plants specifically needed for habitat restoration projects.  

Over the past three years, the Friends of the Koala Nursery has produced over 190,000 plants, including key eucalyptus species (Eucalyptus, Corymbia, Angophora) that are preferred by Koalas in the Lismore region. This grassroots approach supports Koala populations and enhances the resilience of the ecosystem.  

 

 

A Collaborative Effort Creating a Lasting Impact

The success of the Community-Based Nursery Program hinges on collaboration with local Landcare groups, government organisations, and volunteers. By providing high-quality, local plants, FNPW and its partners ensure that habitat restoration projects are ecologically appropriate and sustainable.  

This approach benefits not just Koalas but also other species that share their habitat, such as the Greater Glider (Petauroides Volans), another nationally threatened species. By fostering a diverse range of native plants, the program helps create a balanced and healthy environment that supports a wide array of wildlife. 

 

 

By focusing on habitat restoration and the growth of native Koala food trees, these efforts provide a solid foundation for the recovery of Koala populations in Australia. As part of the National Recovery Plan for the Koala, continued support for community-based nurseries and habitat restoration initiatives is essential. With ongoing collaboration and dedication, there is hope for a better future where Koalas and other species can thrive in their natural habitats.
