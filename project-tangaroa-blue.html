Healing the Land

Tangaroa Blue

Australia-wide   ·   Funded 2018

Eleven million data points from beach clean-ups, turned into plans that stop the rubbish arriving.

11m+Data pointsEntered into the Australian Marine Debris Database since 2004.

Beach clean-ups remove rubbish. They do not, on their own, stop it arriving. The Australian Marine Debris Database exists to close that gap: every volunteer clean-up also records what was found, using one consistent method, so the results add up into a national picture rather than a pile of anecdotes. More than eleven million pieces of data have gone in since 2004.

What we funded

We backed the Tangaroa Blue Foundation in 2018 to do the harder half of the work: analysing that database and turning it into source reduction plans, which means finding where a particular item is entering the environment and stopping it there.

The funding paid for a dedicated Source Reduction Project Officer, and built capacity across the Australian Marine Debris Initiative so project officers could work with communities on developing, running and monitoring their own plans. Volunteers were trained to collect data at local clean-ups, read it to identify what was actually hitting their patch, and then design something that prevented it.

What came of it

Plastic Pollution Working Groups were convened in the New South Wales MidCoast for Browns Creek and Pennington Creek. The Warrnambool community in Victoria collected their own data and set up two source reduction projects from it. Two Northern Rivers clean-up and data activities ran with an artist in residence making installations from what was collected, which reached people the data never would.

Coles and Woolworths collaborated on loading dock litter, a Melbourne community group built a single-use plastic reduction plan, and workshops ran in Gladstone, Airlie Beach, Phillip Island and the Hunter. A new Source Reduction Plan Database now lets a community anywhere look up how someone else already solved the item they are staring at.
