Gifts in wills

Leave a legacy.

A gift in your will keeps the nature you love living on for the generations who come after you. For more than 55 years, gifts in wills have helped protect wild places and the wildlife that calls them home.

The nature you love, living on

Imagine the world your legacy keeps alive.

There is a kind of love that asks for nothing back: a love for places you may never see change, for creatures you will never meet, and for a forest that will stand long after you have gone. A gift in your will for nature is love exactly like that.

Picture the people who come long after us, generations we will never meet, still free to do all the small and ordinary things that make this country feel like home. Picture one of them stopping mid-walk to watch a wedge-tailed eagle ride a thermal across an open sky, or glancing up from the garden as the birds return, season after season, to a country that still has room for them.

A gift in your will helps make certain that world is there, waiting. There are few finer ways to be remembered than as someone who left the future a little wilder, and a great deal more hopeful, than they found it.

A legacy made real

Barbara Harris, and the island her gift helped save.

Some gifts change a single life. Barbara Harris left one that changed an entire landscape.

When Mundoo Island came onto the market after more than a century of farming, its 1,900 hectares of wetland, saltmarsh and coastal habitat at the Murray Mouth had a narrow window to be saved.

A transformative gift from the Estate of the Late Barbara Harris became the foundation of our contribution. Building on it, we worked with the Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal Corporation and the Australian and South Australian Governments to secure the whole island in 2025, and it is now being added to Coorong National Park.

Mundoo is home to 65 nationally listed threatened species and is a refuge for migratory shorebirds on the East Asian-Australasian Flyway. For thousands of years it has held deep cultural meaning for the Ngarrindjeri people, whose connection to this Country continues to guide its care. Where the land was once grazed, restoration has already begun, rebuilding the island’s natural hydrology and healing its wetlands.

None of it would have happened without Barbara. One person, thinking of the future, can protect an entire island for every generation that follows.

What your gift protects

For the ones who cannot speak for themselves.

Every gift left in a will protects something alive and breathing: the yellow-tailed black cockatoo calling across the treetops, the platypus surfacing in a creek brought back from silt and ruin, the quolls and gliders whose scattered homes we are working to reconnect. You can direct your gift to the work closest to your heart, or trust us to place it where the need is greatest.

A story already underway

Fifty-five years of bringing nature back.

A gift in your will joins a story that began on 29 June 1970, written ever since by thousands of hands: Traditional Custodians, rangers, scientists, volunteers, donors and partners, all of them deciding the wild was worth protecting.

  1. 1970

    Within weeks of being founded, we made our first land acquisition, Mount King Station in north-west NSW.

  2. 1979

    We secured Mallee Cliffs National Park, 58,000 hectares of malleefowl habitat in south-west NSW.

  3. 2019

    Alongside our partners, we helped protect 60,000 hectares of Nilpena Station in the Flinders Ranges.

  4. 2020

    After the Black Summer bushfires, we pledged to plant a million trees in five years.

  5. 2024

    We planted our millionth tree, grown across thirty community nurseries.

  6. 2026

    Mundoo Island’s 1,900 hectares, secured in 2025 and made possible by a gift in a will, begin their move into Coorong National Park.

In recent years alone, we have protected and restored almost 30,000 hectares of habitat, far beyond the goal we set ourselves. A gift in your will carries that work forward, into a future none of us will see but all of us are shaping.

Thank you for considering it

No pressure, and no hurry.

To be reading this at all, turning over the idea of giving something of yourself to the natural world, is a quietly remarkable thing. It means that somewhere in the way you think about the years ahead, you have made room for the wild.

A gift in your will is one of the most personal decisions you will ever make, and it deserves time, conversation and care. After you have provided for the people you love, even a small share of what remains can help protect a place for good.

How to leave a gift

Simpler than most people expect.

You can take it one step at a time, and you are free to revisit your decision whenever your circumstances change.

  1. Talk it through with the people you love

    A conversation with family or close friends often brings clarity, and it means your wishes are understood.

  2. Speak with your solicitor or adviser

    They can make sure your will looks after both the people and the causes you care about.

  3. Choose the gift that feels right

    The options below explain the choices, and our suggested wording helps your solicitor write it down.

  4. Let us know, if you would like to

    You are never obliged to tell us, but it lets us thank you and understand your wishes.

Residual gift

What remains of your estate once loved ones, debts and specific gifts are settled. It never takes from anyone else you have named.

Percentage gift

A share of your estate, so your gift stays in proportion with everything else you leave, whatever the final value.

Specific sum

A set amount that feels right to you. It is worth reviewing every few years, as inflation changes what it can do.

Shares or other assets

A specific asset such as shares or artwork. Land can’t be left in a will, but you can give land in your lifetime.

You can also give through a trust, which your adviser can help you arrange, or leave a gift for a specific purpose, such as acquiring land for a national park or protecting a particular species. Please talk to us first so we can make sure your wishes can be fulfilled.

For you and your solicitor

Suggested wording for your will.

If you would like to include a gift to us, your solicitor may find the following wording helpful. Please confirm the details with us or with your solicitor before finalising.

I give to the Foundation for National Parks & Wildlife (ABN 90 107 744 771) of Level 6, 10 Bridge Street, Sydney NSW 2000, [a percentage of my estate / the residue of my estate / the sum of $______ / the following specific gift] for its general purposes. I direct that the receipt of the Chief Executive Officer or authorised officer shall be a sufficient discharge to my executors.

A note on tax

For many people, a gift in their will is a tax-effective way to give, and as a registered charity with Deductible Gift Recipient status we are able to receive gifts of this kind. Everyone’s circumstances are different, so we always recommend talking it through with your solicitor or financial adviser.

Frequently asked

Questions about gifts in wills.

Do I need to tell you if I leave a gift in my will?
No, you are never obliged to. If you feel comfortable letting us know, it allows us to thank you and to understand your wishes properly. Your decision stays private, and entirely yours.
Can I choose what my gift supports?
Yes. You can dedicate your gift to a cause or region that means something to you, such as protecting native species or healing a particular landscape, or you can trust us to direct it wherever the need is greatest. If you would like to direct your gift, please talk to us first so we can make sure your wishes can be fulfilled.
How will my gift be used?
Your gift supports the work at the core of what we do: expanding protected areas, restoring ecosystems and protecting threatened species. In 2025, 80 per cent of our funds were invested in our core purpose.
Do I need a great deal of money to leave a gift?
Not at all. A gift of any size makes a real and lasting difference.
Can I leave land to you in my will?
No. Land can’t be left to us in a will, but you can give land in your lifetime so it becomes national park. Find out about donating land.
Can I change my mind later?
Of course. Your will is always yours to update as your life changes. If you already have a will, your solicitor can advise whether a codicil (a formal amendment) or a new will is the better way to add a gift.
How do you work with Traditional Owners?
Respectfully, and always in partnership. We work alongside Traditional Custodians to care for Country through shared decision-making and co-design, so that cultural knowledge and leadership guide how land and species are protected.
