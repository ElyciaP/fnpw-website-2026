Nilpena National Park

Flinders Ranges, South Australia   ·   Proclaimed 2021

Sixty thousand hectares of the Flinders Ranges, holding the world’s richest record of the first complex life on Earth.

60,000HectaresSecured and proclaimed as national park in June 2021.
111Native plant speciesRecorded across the park, including two threatened communities.
44Native animal speciesSheltered by mulga low woodland and river red gum country.

Nilpena sits in the Flinders Ranges on Adnyamathanha Country, and it holds something no other place holds in the same condition. The fossil beds preserved there are the richest and most diverse record of the Ediacaran period anywhere in the world, capturing the emergence of complex animal life across roughly 94 million years.

How the park was made

We funded the acquisition of Nilpena Station in 2019 through our Growing Parks program, alongside the Flinders Ranges Ediacara Foundation and the South Australian Government. On 17 June 2021 the land was proclaimed as Nilpena Ediacara National Park.

What the park protects is not only geological. It supports 111 native plant species and 44 native animal species, and two threatened ecological communities sit within its boundaries: river red gum woodland and mulga low woodland. In April 2021 the site was added to Australia’s tentative list for World Heritage nomination.
