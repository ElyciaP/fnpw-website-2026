Growing Resilience from the Ground Up

February 2026   ·   South Australia

Restoring native vegetation is one of the most practical and powerful ways we can help heal land, rebuild biodiversity, and create healthier habitats for wildlife and communities.

But successful restoration begins long before the first tree is planted.

It begins with seed.

This is where Trees For Life plays such an important role. Based in South Australia, they help grow the native seed and plants that restoration projects across the country depend on.

Together, through our Landscape Resilience Program, we’re supporting seed collection and nursery efforts that make recovery possible.

Why seed

Why seeds matter in restoration

Every healthy ecosystem starts with the right plants in the right place.

When we restore degraded bushland, reconnect fragmented habitats, or revegetate land after fire or flood, we rely on native seedstock to bring these landscapes back to life.

Seed is not simply a supply issue. It is a biodiversity issue.

In ecological restoration, it is essential that plants come from the local region and are adapted to local soils, rainfall patterns, temperatures, and relationships with wildlife. This is known as local provenance, and it plays a critical role in long-term survival and ecosystem health.

Without enough locally appropriate seed, restoration efforts slow down, projects are delayed, and biodiversity outcomes are compromised.

Australia is currently facing a growing shortage of native seed, particularly for region-specific and hard-to-grow species that are vital for healthy bushland recovery.

FNPW’s Landscape Resilience Program was created in response to this challenge, helping increase the supply of native, local provenance plants needed for ecological restoration across the country.

Collection

Seed collection: a critical first step

Seed collection is one of the most important and least visible parts of restoration.

It involves carefully gathering seed from native plants in the wild to ensure:

  • genetic diversity is maintained
  • local ecosystems are properly represented
  • rare and threatened species can be propagated
  • restoration plantings are suited to the landscapes they return to

Ethical seed collection takes planning, expertise and patience. It requires working with seasons, understanding plant lifecycles, and collecting in ways that protect wild populations.

Within the Landscape Resilience Program, increasing the volume, diversity and access to native seedstock is a key priority for long-term ecological recovery.

Trees For Life plays a vital role in making this possible.

The partner

Trees For Life: expertise, community, and local impact

Trees For Life has worked for decades to restore South Australia’s landscapes through seed-based restoration, nursery propagation, and hands-on community action.

A cornerstone of their work is their commitment to seed collection and local provenance, ensuring restoration projects do not just plant more vegetation, but restore ecosystems that truly belong to place.

This approach supports stronger habitats, healthier biodiversity, and better long-term outcomes for wildlife.

The nursery

A nursery growing biodiversity, species by species

At the heart of Trees For Life’s impact is their remarkable nursery.

Each year, Trees For Life grows more than 300 different indigenous species of trees, shrubs, grasses and groundcovers. This diversity is essential for restoring functioning ecosystems, not just planting trees.

Unlike many commercial nurseries, Trees For Life is prepared to invest in:

  • locally sourced provenance seed
  • hard-to-grow native species
  • specialised restoration orders
  • ecological outcomes over convenience

Their plants are grown, propagated and nurtured by experienced nursery staff, alongside the support of volunteers who give their time to help restore the landscapes they care about.

This kind of capacity is critical to meeting Australia’s restoration needs.

Our Landscape Resilience Program works directly with existing nurseries and supports the establishment of new hubs to increase the number of plants and species that can be grown for restoration projects.

Trees For Life is a leading example of what this looks like in practice.

The program

A shared role in the Landscape Resilience Program

Our Landscape Resilience Program is a national effort to restore ecosystems in climate-impacted and degraded landscapes, improving resilience where it is needed most.

The program brings together community nurseries, seed collection initiatives, restoration partners and volunteers to strengthen the restoration supply chain and help landscapes recover.

Through our partnership with Trees For Life, we are helping support the growing and planting of native vegetation that will:

  • rebuild habitat after disturbance
  • strengthen biodiversity in high-risk landscapes
  • restore ecological connectivity
  • support wildlife and communities into the future

This work is practical, long-term, and deeply place-based.

It starts with seed, and it continues through the care, knowledge and effort of organisations like Trees For Life.

How you can help

Working together to restore what has been lost

You can be part of this effort too.

When you donate a tree, you help grow and plant native species where they are needed most, supporting the recovery of landscapes impacted by fire, flood and climate extremes.

Your gift contributes directly to our goal of planting 9 million trees by 2030, helping restore habitat, reconnect ecosystems, and give wildlife the space to return.

Every tree begins with a seed, and every donation helps that seed become something lasting.
