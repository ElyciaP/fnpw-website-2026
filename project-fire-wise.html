The problem

Clearing the bush does not make it safer

Every summer, native vegetation gets cleared on the belief that it feeds fire. The instinct is understandable and it costs us twice. Habitat disappears, and the bare ground left behind is often more dangerous than what was taken out of it. Open, cleared country carries fire fastest: a grass fire can travel at three times the ground speed of a fire moving through forest.

The science is more particular than the instinct allows. Close to a building, fire turns on radiant heat, wind speed and ember attack, and embers are the most common reason houses catch alight.

The approach

Built with the people already doing the work

We started Fire Wise in 2023 because the knowledge needed to act on this is local, and so is the plant stock. We partner with fourteen community groups across Victoria, South Australia and New South Wales, among them the South Gippsland Landcare Network, Lakes Entrance Landcare, the Alexandrina Community Nursery and the volunteers at Panboola Wetlands.

Around them sits a wider group we could not do this without: local councils, Traditional Owners, ecologists and botanists who know their own country, fire service agencies, and researchers at CSIRO who keep the plant selection anchored to evidence rather than folklore. Our role is to fund the work, connect the people doing it to each other, and make sure what one group learns does not stay in one valley.

The outcome

What the first phase left behind

This phase has now concluded and the result is legible. Thousands of low-flammability natives are established, twelve community nurseries are stronger than we found them, and thirty-eight species are now identified and supported as Fire Wise plantings.

The plants matter. The capability matters more. A community that grows its own local stock, and knows which species belong where, is equipped to revegetate after a fire rather than only to brace for one. That is the outcome we were after: protection for homes and landscapes that is not paid for in biodiversity.
