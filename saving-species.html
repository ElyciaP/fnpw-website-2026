Why it matters

What this work does

Australia is home to plants and animals found nowhere else on Earth, and one of the worst extinction records anywhere. These projects fund the unglamorous, essential work of recovery: breeding programs, monitoring, nest boxes, disease treatment and the science that underpins all of it.

From koalas and quolls to enigma moths and endangered wattles, if it is on the brink, this is the work fighting for it.

How we do it

  • We fund recovery programs run with researchers, carers and land managers
  • We back both flagship species and the overlooked ones
  • Every project reports real outcomes: populations, hectares, hollows, seedlings
Featured project

Warddeken Mayh Recovery Project

Indigenous rangers run a network of permanent camera monitoring sites across 1.4 million hectares of the Warddeken Indigenous Protected Area, recovering threatened mammals through right-way fire and feral animal management.

It is the largest single landscape we work across and the clearest demonstration of what this work funds: local knowledge, long timeframes, measured outcomes.

Bringing Back the Growling Grass Frog

The Growling Grass Frog is listed as Vulnerable and has vanished from much of its former range. At Winton Wetlands in north-east Victoria, a founding population of 60 to 65 frogs is now in place, with monitoring running to March 2026 to see whether they breed.
