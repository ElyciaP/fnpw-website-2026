Ways to partner

Fund Conservation at Scale

Fund the acquisition of land for national parks, the growing and planting of native habitat, or recovery work for a threatened species. Every project starts with an agreed scope and what your funding will achieve.

Our projects: Mundoo Island Station, Remarkable Southern Flinders

Build Conservation Partnerships

The most lasting results take years, not a single funding round. We build long-term partnerships around a landscape, a species or a cause that matters to you, and grow them as the results come in.

Our projects: Landscape Resilience Program, Native Plant Nurseries, L’OCCITANE Australia

Invest in Nature’s Future

We’re exploring how public, philanthropic and private funding could work together on long-term landscape outcomes, and we’d welcome a conversation with organisations who want to help shape it.

Corporate
L’OCCITANE Australia · since 2020

Restoring a Ramsar wetland on Hindmarsh Island

L’OCCITANE has partnered with us since 2020, when they helped fund our first Bushfire Recovery Nurseries. In 2025 their $90,000 contribution helped restore coastal woodland and wetland across five sites on Hindmarsh Island (Kumarangk), alongside the Ngarrindjeri, the Kumarangk Hindmarsh Island Landcare Group, local nurseries and park managers.

NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service

Curb Wombat Mange Program

Together we put up to $2 million into rapid response grants between 2023 and 2025, getting funding to Indigenous community organisations and licensed wildlife groups across NSW. An estimated 1,498 wombats were treated in the first large-scale targeted program of its kind in the state.

Philanthropy
Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal Corporation
With the Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal Corporation and the Australian and SA Governments

Mundoo Island Station

When this rare land at the Murray Mouth came up for sale, we joined the Australian and South Australian Governments as an acquisition partner, with a substantial part of our contribution coming from a gift in a will. The islands are now being added to Coorong National Park, protecting habitat for 65 nationally listed threatened species, and their management plan is being developed with the Ngarrindjeri Aboriginal Corporation.

How we work

How a partnership runs.

  1. 01

    Identify

    With Traditional Custodians, scientists and government, we find the places and species where a partnership will make the biggest difference, and shape them into projects ready for funding.

  2. 02

    Deliver

    We bring the partners and funding together, then engage specialists on the ground, from Traditional Owner ranger groups to community nurseries and wildlife organisations, against clear milestones.

  3. 03

    Demonstrate

    We track the ecological and financial outcomes and report back, so you can see what your partnership achieved and where it could go next.
